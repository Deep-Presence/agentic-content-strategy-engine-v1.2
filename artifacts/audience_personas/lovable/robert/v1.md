# Persona: The Boutique Consulting Founder (Under Siege)

---

## 1. Persona Title & Snapshot

**Persona Title:** The Boutique Consulting Founder (Under Siege)

**Name & Snapshot:**

Robert runs a twelve-person strategy consulting firm generating $3.2M annually, serving mid-market clients in financial services and healthcare. After fourteen years building the practice from scratch, he's now watching competitors win RFPs partly because they can demonstrate SOC 2 compliance and formal cybersecurity programs—checkboxes Robert's firm cannot yet tick. His clients entrust him with merger plans, competitive intelligence, and proprietary financial models, yet his "security infrastructure" consists of LastPass, Dropbox Business, and a part-time bookkeeper who also handles IT questions.

---

## 2. Daily Reality *(7 bullets)*

* Juggles client delivery, business development, and operations with no dedicated IT staff—security decisions fall between cracks.
* Relies on a mix of contractors, remote employees, and offshore designers working on personal laptops via coffee shop WiFi.
* Stores client deliverables, pitch decks, and strategic frameworks across Dropbox, Google Drive, Notion, and individual email accounts.
* Receives compliance questionnaires from enterprise clients asking about penetration testing, incident response plans, and data encryption protocols—has no answers.
* Spends evenings Googling "small business cybersecurity checklist" and feels overwhelmed by conflicting advice requiring technical expertise he lacks.
* Watches insurance premiums climb after broker mentions "professional services cyber liability" now requires documented security controls to avoid coverage gaps.
* Loses an average of four hours weekly to password resets, file permission conflicts, and contractor onboarding/offboarding across disconnected tools.

---

## 3. Core Fears *(5 bullets)*

* A single stolen laptop or phishing attack exposes three years of client strategy documents, triggering lawsuit and practice collapse.
* Major client conducts vendor audit, discovers security gaps, and terminates contract—then tells industry peers why.
* Ransomware locks all project files two days before critical client presentation with no backup recovery plan.
* Former contractor retains access to client data months after project ends because no systematic offboarding process exists.
* Insurance claim denied after breach because policy requires "reasonable security measures" firm cannot demonstrate having implemented.

---

## 4. Deep Motivations *(4 bullets)*

* Protect the reputation built over fourteen years—client trust is the only moat against commoditization and price competition.
* Sleep knowing client data is genuinely secure, not just hoping nothing bad happens before someone else's problem becomes visible.
* Win enterprise RFPs requiring vendor security attestations without hiring full-time IT staff or spending $150K on compliance consultants.
* Exit the practice in 5-7 years with valuation intact, not diminished by cybersecurity incidents or unaddressed technical debt.

---

## 5. Trust Builders *(5 bullets)*

* Show me a compliance certification roadmap requiring zero technical expertise—just follow documented steps and you're SOC 2 ready in 90 days.
* Prove your solution actually works for firms like mine with case studies from other professional services firms (not tech startups).
* Demonstrate "set-it-and-forget-it" automation—I shouldn't need to remember monthly tasks or understand technical jargon to stay protected.
* Provide insurance carrier endorsements or premium reductions proving your security controls meet underwriter standards and reduce liability.
* Offer a single throat to choke when something breaks—no finger-pointing between six different vendors claiming it's someone else's problem.

---

## 6. Trust Killers *(5 bullets)*

* Requiring me to understand the difference between SIEM, EDR, MDR, and XDR before making a purchase decision.
* "Free trials" that require configuring integrations, provisioning infrastructure, or reading 47-page implementation guides before seeing value.
* Pricing that scales unpredictably with users, devices, or data volume—I need fixed costs I can budget against.
* Solutions designed for 500+ employee enterprises with features I'll never use and complexity I can't maintain.
* Vendor sales processes requiring multiple stakeholder meetings, technical evaluations, and procurement workflows designed for Fortune 500 buyers.

---

## 7. Critical Pain Points *(7 bullets)*

* No systematic process for contractor offboarding means former team members retain access to client files indefinitely until someone remembers.
* Client compliance questionnaires ask for documentation (incident response plans, vendor risk assessments, penetration test reports) that simply doesn't exist.
* Data scattered across personal and business accounts with no centralized visibility into who can access what client information.
* Zero confidence that existing backup procedures would actually work in a ransomware scenario—never tested recovery in three years.
* Competitive disadvantage in enterprise RFPs explicitly requiring SOC 2, ISO 27001, or documented security frameworks as table stakes.
* Cyber insurance premiums rising 30% annually while coverage shrinks because carrier questionnaires expose gaps in security controls.
* No technical expertise to evaluate whether current tools (Dropbox, Google Workspace, LastPass) actually meet client contractual obligations around data protection.

---
